Adaptive Learning Prototype

What Would You Say?

A rule-based adaptive learning experience.

Some conversations are harder than others.
Respond as you would in the moment.
As the conversation continues, the next prompt will shift based on how you respond.

There is no script to follow.
The goal is to notice what you do, what changes, and what you might try differently next time.

How responses are assessed

What does a 1–4 score mean?

Responses are assessed across five skills. The prototype looks for observable behaviors in what the learner says and uses those results to determine what the learner practices next.

1
Needs development
The behavior is missing, unclear, or works against the conversation.
2
Developing
The behavior is present, but incomplete, generic, or inconsistent.
3
Proficient
The behavior is clear, appropriate, and supports the conversation.
4
Strong
The behavior is specific, responsive to the customer, and helps move the conversation forward effectively.
Empathy
1 — Does not acknowledge the customer's frustration or emotion.
2 — Uses a general acknowledgment, but it could apply to almost any situation.
3 — Specifically acknowledges the customer's frustration and connects it to what happened.
4 — Recognizes both the customer's emotion and why the situation created that reaction before moving toward resolution.
Listening
1 — Misses or misinterprets the main concern.
2 — Responds to the general issue but does not reference important details.
3 — Accurately responds to at least one specific concern or detail the customer raised.
4 — Demonstrates understanding of both the immediate issue and the customer's underlying concern.
Ownership
1 — Deflects responsibility or leaves the customer unclear about who will help.
2 — Offers assistance but relies heavily on process language or another team.
3 — Clearly states what they will personally do to move the issue forward.
4 — Takes clear ownership, explains what will happen next, and sets realistic expectations for follow-through.
Problem Solving
1 — Does not offer a meaningful next step.
2 — Offers a vague or incomplete solution.
3 — Gives a specific, realistic next step that addresses the problem.
4 — Provides a clear solution, explains what happens next, and addresses potential barriers or uncertainty.
Communication
1 — The response is confusing, abrupt, overly defensive, or difficult to follow.
2 — Generally understandable but may be too vague, lengthy, scripted, or unclear.
3 — Clear, calm, concise, and professional.
4 — Clear and concise while also sounding natural, confident, and responsive to the customer's situation.
This is a rule-based prototype. The scoring uses observable words, phrases, response length, and combinations of behaviors as proxies for the rubric. A future AI-enabled version could evaluate meaning and context more deeply.
